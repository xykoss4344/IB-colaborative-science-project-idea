HELIOS-NET GEOPHYSICAL ANALYSIS

Document Classification: SECURED GRID Briefing

Project HELIOS-NET functions as an active thermo-optical planetary filter. The system intercepts convective thermal plumes and upwelling longwave radiation, venting a fraction off-world to mitigate greenhouse forcing.

1. Atmospheric Transparency Window

The Earth's greenhouse gases (CO₂, H₂O, CH₄) absorb most thermal IR emitted by the crust. However, the atmosphere is transparent between 8.0 - 13.0 μm (the atmospheric infrared window). HELIOS-NET space nodes orbit above this window, intercepting escaping photons.

2. Thermodynamic Equations

  • A. Stefan-Boltzmann Emission Upwelling energy flux $j^*$ radiated from the Earth's surface is calculated as:
    j* = ε σ T⁴
    where $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\text{K}^4$. At $288.15\text{ K}$, this generates $390.1\text{ W/m}^2$ of upwelling energy.
  • B. Carnot Thermal Gradient limit thermoelectric generators on the LEO nodes operate between hot Earth emission plates and cold space radiator plates. Maximum efficiency is limited by:
    η_max = 1 - (Tc / Th)
    With space temperature $T_c \approx 3\text{ K}$, the theoretical thermodynamic limit is $98.9\%$, enabling efficient solid-state thermoelectric generation.

3. Vector Energy Routing

By exporting 70% of harvested heat into deep space via Nd:YAG lasers, the system effectively breaks the greenhouse gas energy blanket, lowering the global equilibrium temperature offset. The remaining 30% is beamed to ground rectennas via microwaves, powering the planetary grid.
